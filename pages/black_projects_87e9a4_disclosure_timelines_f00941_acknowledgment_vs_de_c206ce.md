## Introduction

Officially admitting that a secret programme exists is not the same as declassifying it. In practice, governments often acknowledge the existence of a previously hidden aircraft, satellite, intelligence capability or military programme while continuing to protect the information that gives it operational value. The result is an "acknowledgment gap": the public knows that the project is real, but many of the most important facts remain legally classified and practically inaccessible.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/black_projects_87e9a4_disclosure_timelines_f00941_acknowledgment_vs_de_c206ce-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Acknowledgment Gap illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/black_projects_87e9a4_disclosure_timelines_f00941_acknowledgment_vs_de_c206ce-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/black_projects_87e9a4_disclosure_timelines_f00941_acknowledgment_vs_de_c206ce-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This staged approach allows governments to resolve the political or practical need to admit a programme without surrendering intelligence advantages. Public confirmation may reveal little more than a name, a photograph or a broad mission statement, while detailed capabilities, operational history, supporting infrastructure and technical documentation remain protected for years or even decades.

## The legal and practical meaning of official acknowledgment

Official acknowledgment is a narrow legal concept rather than a general statement that "the secret is out". In the United States, courts have consistently distinguished between rumours, leaks, investigative journalism and authorised government disclosures. Information enters the public domain in a legal sense only when an authorised agency officially releases or confirms it. Even widespread reporting or unofficial admissions do not necessarily remove classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/FOIA/DF-2020-00129-Glomar-Paper.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DF 2020 00129 Glomar Paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National IntelligenceApproved for release by ODNI on 04/29/2024, FOIA Case # DF-2020-00129December 29, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction has important consequences for access to records. A government may publicly display an aircraft at an air show yet still refuse to release documents describing its [radar signature]({{ 'radar-signature/' | relative_url }}), software, mission planning or sensor performance. Likewise, officials may confirm that a reconnaissance programme existed without confirming every document connected to it.

The doctrine also explains why governments can continue to issue "neither confirm nor deny" (NCND), or "Glomar", responses for some requests even after related information has become public. A requester must usually show that the specific information sought—not merely the general subject—has already been officially acknowledged. A broad admission that an intelligence activity exists does not automatically require disclosure of individual operations, records or methods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/ogis/resources/foia-ombuds-observer/2024-01" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

## Which facts remain protected after confirmation?

Public acknowledgment normally redraws the secrecy boundary rather than removing it altogether. The government decides which layer of information has become acceptable to reveal while preserving information whose disclosure could still affect current operations or future capabilities.

Common categories that often remain classified include:

* **Technical performance**, including speed, range, endurance, radar cross-section, electronic warfare capability and sensor resolution.
* **Operational employment**, such as target selection, mission profiles, deployment patterns and rules for use.
* **Supporting systems**, including communications networks, logistics, software, encryption, [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) procedures and mission-planning tools.
* **[Intelligence sources]({{ 'intelligence-sources/' | relative_url }}) and methods**, covering collection techniques, processing methods and analytical capabilities.
* **Industrial relationships**, including contractor roles, specialised manufacturing techniques and classified supply chains.
* **Budget structure**, particularly when expenditure reveals priorities, production rates or future procurement plans.

In many cases these supporting systems are more sensitive than the platform itself. Once an aircraft has been photographed, its existence may no longer justify complete secrecy. However, knowledge of exactly how it detects targets, exchanges information with other assets or penetrates defended [airspace]({{ 'airspace/' | relative_url }}) may still provide valuable intelligence to potential adversaries.

## How partial releases redraw the secrecy boundary

Governments often disclose information in carefully controlled stages, each designed to satisfy a particular objective while retaining maximum control over sensitive details.

The first stage may simply acknowledge that a programme exists. Later releases may include selected photographs, official designations or limited historical narratives. Technical reports, engineering documents and operational records may remain classified for decades afterwards.

This approach allows agencies to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--benefit" markdown="1">

* reduce speculation that has become unsustainable;
* prepare public funding or procurement decisions;
* reassure allies or deter adversaries through selective disclosure;
* preserve military advantages by withholding performance data.

</div>

Importantly, each release establishes a new but limited public baseline. Everything outside that authorised disclosure may remain classified even if observers can reasonably infer additional details.

## Case studies showing the acknowledgment gap

### The F-117 Nighthawk

The Lockheed [F-117]({{ 'f-117/' | relative_url }}) illustrates the difference between acknowledgment and declassification. The aircraft first flew in 1981 and entered operational service several years before the US Air Force released an official photograph in 1988. The public acknowledgment confirmed the aircraft's existence, but stealth design principles, operational tactics, radar cross-section characteristics and mission planning remained closely protected. Even after combat use in Panama and the Gulf War, many technical aspects of the programme continued to be classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/declassification.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Declassified Records &#124; National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesDeclassified Records &#124; National ArchivesMay 28, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2020</span></span></span>

The public therefore learned that the aircraft existed long before it gained access to the engineering and operational knowledge that made the programme strategically significant.

### National Reconnaissance Office satellite programmes

The National Reconnaissance Office provides an even clearer example of staged disclosure. It has declassified major historical reconnaissance programmes such as CORONA and released extensive historical documentation. At the same time, the agency explicitly states that classified information still exists concerning these programmes and that many other NRO programmes remain entirely classified, including in some cases even their names. The continued protection reflects concerns that historical details may reveal enduring collection methods or assist foreign intelligence services in understanding current capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nro.gov/foia-home/Declassified-NRO-Programs-and-Projects/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nro.gov">[National Reconnaissance Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nro.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nro.gov.</span></span></span>

This demonstrates that even formal declassification of a programme does not necessarily declassify every associated fact.

## Why secrecy often survives after the technology is visible

Many observers assume that once an aircraft or spacecraft has been photographed, little secrecy remains. In reality, visual observation frequently [reveals]({{ 'reveals/' | relative_url }}) only a small fraction of what makes the system valuable.

Modern military capability increasingly depends upon characteristics that cannot be determined from external appearance alone, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* sensor fusion software;
* electronic warfare techniques;
* signal processing;
* secure communications;
* artificial intelligence support systems;
* integration with classified command networks.

</div>

Because these features may remain relevant for decades, agencies often judge that continued classification still provides genuine operational protection even after the hardware itself has become publicly known.

Historical programmes also create continuing risks. Detailed engineering decisions from an older platform may illuminate design philosophies, manufacturing methods or intelligence priorities that remain embedded in current systems.

## Why Freedom of Information requests often still fail

A common misunderstanding is that official confirmation should automatically open all associated files under freedom of information laws. In practice, disclosure systems operate document by document rather than programme by programme.

A requester seeking additional records must overcome existing classification exemptions for each category of information. Courts generally require that the requested information match a previous official disclosure very closely. General public knowledge, media reporting or statements from different government organisations usually do not satisfy this standard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/FOIA/DF-2020-00129-Glomar-Paper.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DF 2020 00129 Glomar Paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National IntelligenceApproved for release by ODNI on 04/29/2024, FOIA Case # DF-2020-00129December 29, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2024</span></span></span>

As a result, agencies may lawfully release historical summaries while continuing to withhold technical annexes, operational reports, intelligence assessments or supporting documentation.

## What the acknowledgment gap reveals about black projects

The acknowledgment gap reflects the fact that secrecy is rarely an all-or-nothing condition. [Black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}) usually transition through several stages in which the public learns progressively more about a programme, but each stage establishes a new boundary rather than eliminating secrecy altogether.

Official acknowledgment therefore serves multiple purposes simultaneously. It can end speculation over whether a programme exists, provide political transparency, support procurement decisions or reinforce deterrence, while still preserving the classified knowledge that governments believe would most benefit potential adversaries. The result is a long period during which a programme is publicly recognised yet remains, in legally and operationally important respects, a classified project.

        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
