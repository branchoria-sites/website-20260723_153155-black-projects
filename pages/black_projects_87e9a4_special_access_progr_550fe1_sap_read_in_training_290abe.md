## Introduction

Being "read into" a Special Access Program (SAP) is more than receiving permission to view highly classified information. It is a formal security process that combines legal acknowledgements, programme-specific indoctrination and continuing training designed to ensure that every authorised individual understands exactly what information is protected, how it must be handled and what obligations continue throughout their access. Within [black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}), this process is a central mechanism for controlling secrecy because access depends not only on clearance and [need-to-know]({{ 'need-to-know/' | relative_url }}), but also on completing programme-specific security requirements and maintaining them over time. Executive Order 13526 authorises SAPs to impose safeguarding and access requirements beyond those normally required for information at the same classification level, providing the legal foundation for these enhanced obligations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Training/eLearning/SA001-resources/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Course Resources for Special Access Programs (SAP) Overview SA001.16</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Course Resources for Special Access Programs (SAP) Overview SA001.16</span></span></span>

## What happens when someone is read in?

A SAP read-in is a structured indoctrination process rather than a simple administrative [approval]({{ 'approval/' | relative_url }}). Before gaining access, an individual must already meet the programme's eligibility requirements, after which the programme security office conducts an official briefing explaining the programme's specific security rules.

The Department of Defense's 2025 SAP Security Manual requires individuals to sign a SAP Indoctrination Agreement before or during indoctrination. The briefing is intended to familiarise newly authorised personnel with the programme's critical protected information, unique handling procedures, reporting obligations and the consequences of unauthorised disclosure. Unlike a general security clearance briefing, the emphasis is on the specific risks and controls associated with that individual programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs &amp; Templates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

The read-in therefore serves several purposes simultaneously:

## Indoctrination agreements and security paperwork

Read-in paperwork is intended to establish [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) rather than simply collect signatures. Multiple documents may be involved depending on the programme, organisation and whether government personnel or [contractors]({{ 'contractors/' | relative_url }}) are concerned.

Common elements include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **SAP Indoctrination Agreement**, documenting acceptance of programme-specific security obligations.
* **Classified Information Nondisclosure Agreement (Standard Form 312)**, the government-wide agreement covering protection of classified information.
* **Programme Access Request** and associated approval documentation demonstrating that access was properly authorised.
* **Training records** showing completion of required SAP security instruction.
* **Personnel questionnaires or screening documentation**, where required by the programme's nomination process.

</div>

The Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) maintains repositories of approved SAP forms and templates for government organisations and cleared contractors. These include programme access requests, pre-screening questionnaires, SAP training record templates and related administrative documents that support consistent implementation of the DoD SAP Security Manual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs &amp; Templates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

The existence of standard templates illustrates an important feature of SAP administration: although individual programmes differ, the process of documenting access is highly standardised to support oversight and inspections.

## Initial and annual SAP security training

Read-in is only the beginning of the security education process. DoD policy requires both initial and recurring security training for everyone with SAP access.

Initial training normally accompanies the first programme indoctrination and explains subjects such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* programme-specific classification guidance;
* handling, storage and transmission rules;
* reporting responsibilities for security incidents;
* insider threat awareness;
* foreign travel or foreign contact reporting where applicable;
* safeguarding critical programme information.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Training/Special-Access-Programs/about/catalog/sap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Special Access Program is established for a specific class of classified information that imposes safeguarding and access requirements...</span></span></span>

</div>

The SAP Security Manual also requires annual refresher training. The objective is not simply to repeat previous material but to reinforce security awareness, remind personnel of continuing obligations and address changes affecting the programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs &amp; Templates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

The DCSA and the Center for Development of Security Excellence (CDSE) support this approach through SAP-specific training courses, instructor-led seminars, job aids and security awareness materials covering SAP roles, briefings, safeguarding procedures and security incidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Training/Toolkits/Special-Access-Program-Toolkit/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Program Toolkit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Toolkit</span></span></span>

## When training must be updated

SAP security education is designed to respond to changing risks rather than follow a fixed schedule alone.

The current DoD SAP Security Manual states that training should be updated whenever significant changes affect the programme, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* revised security classification guidance;
* changes to protected programme information;
* changes in whether the programme is acknowledged or unacknowledged;
* emerging threats or vulnerabilities affecting programme security.

</div>

This requirement reflects a practical reality of black projects: technologies, intelligence assessments and adversary capabilities evolve. Personnel therefore need updated guidance whenever the security environment changes rather than waiting for the next annual refresher.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs &amp; Templates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

For example, if new intelligence indicates that a foreign intelligence service is targeting a particular technology area, programme security officers may issue additional awareness briefings, modify reporting requirements or reinforce restrictions on discussions, travel or electronic device use.

## Why documentation matters as much as the briefing

From a security management perspective, paperwork is not merely administrative overhead. It provides evidence that programme security requirements have been properly implemented.

Inspection authorities can verify that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* only approved individuals received access;
* required indoctrinations occurred before access was granted;
* annual refresher training was completed;
* debriefings were conducted when access ended;
* records support the programme's access control decisions.

</div>

The DCSA's published SAP templates include training record forms and inspection checklists that help programmes demonstrate compliance during security reviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs &amp; Templates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

This documentation also supports continuity. Personnel may transfer between organisations, contractors may change, and programmes can evolve over many years. Accurate records help ensure that access decisions remain consistent and auditable.

## Read-out and continuing obligations

Security responsibilities do not end when an individual leaves a SAP. Programme personnel are normally formally debriefed, sometimes described as being "read out" of the programme.

The debriefing reminds departing personnel that:

* nondisclosure obligations continue indefinitely unless information is officially [declassified]({{ 'declassified/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* classified knowledge cannot be discussed with unauthorised persons;
* programme materials must be returned or properly accounted for;
* future contacts or requests for information may need to be reported.

</div>

CDSE guidance treats briefings, refresher training and debriefings as connected stages within the lifecycle of SAP access rather than isolated administrative events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Training/Job-Aids/Special-Access-Programs-Job-Aids/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Programs Job Aids</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs Job Aids</span></span></span>

## Why repeated security education is central to SAP secrecy

The effectiveness of a Special Access Program depends on more than selecting trustworthy personnel. It depends on continually reinforcing a shared understanding of exactly what information is protected, how adversaries may seek it and what behaviours are expected from everyone entrusted with access.

The read-in process creates the initial legal and administrative framework for that [trust]({{ 'trust/' | relative_url }}). Regular training, updated guidance and carefully maintained records ensure that programme secrecy remains an active security discipline throughout the life of a black project, rather than a one-time clearance decision.
