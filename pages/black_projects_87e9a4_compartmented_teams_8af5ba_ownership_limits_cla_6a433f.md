## Introduction

A company can possess the technical expertise to build some of the world's most sensitive defence or intelligence systems yet still be prevented from giving its own senior executives unrestricted access to the work. In the United States, this occurs when a cleared contractor is judged to be under **Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence (FOCI)**. Rather than automatically excluding foreign-owned firms from classified programmes, the government often allows participation only after imposing governance structures that separate the classified business from the normal corporate chain of command. These arrangements are particularly significant for Special Access Programs (SAPs) and other highly compartmented projects, where ownership can determine not only whether a company wins work but also which individuals inside the company are permitted to know what.

Within the broader world of special-access [contractors]({{ 'contractors/' | relative_url }}) and compartmented engineering teams, ownership restrictions create an additional layer of [compartmentation]({{ 'compartmentation/' | relative_url }}). The security boundary is no longer just between different engineering groups; it may also run between the cleared subsidiary and its own parent company.

## Why foreign ownership becomes a security issue

The central concern is not foreign investment by itself. US policy explicitly recognises that foreign capital can strengthen the defence industrial base. The issue is whether a foreign shareholder could direct company decisions or gain access to classified information through ordinary corporate authority. The Defence Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) defines FOCI as a situation where a foreign interest has the power—whether or not exercised—to influence management or operations in a way that could expose classified information or adversely affect classified contracts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/FOCI/When/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence...</span></span></span>

## When a cleared company cannot trust its own hierarchy

One of the least intuitive consequences of FOCI mitigation is that ordinary corporate authority may stop at the boundary of the cleared subsidiary.

In a conventional multinational corporation, senior executives expect visibility into budgets, engineering priorities, programme risks and major contracts throughout the organisation. For highly classified work, that expectation may be deliberately broken. A foreign parent company can remain the legal owner while being prevented from receiving classified programme details, participating in certain management decisions or directing cleared operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Entity-Vetting-Facility-Clearances-FOCI/Foreign-Ownership-Control-or-Influence/Mitigation-Agreements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mitigation Agreements</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mitigation Agreements...</span></span></span>

This means:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Parent-company executives may not receive classified technical briefings.
* Foreign-appointed directors may be excluded from sensitive discussions.
* Programme managers may report through security-governed channels rather than ordinary corporate reporting lines.
* Cleared personnel can owe legal security obligations that override normal corporate information-sharing expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Entity-Vetting-Facility-Clearances-FOCI/Foreign-Ownership-Control-or-Influence/Mitigation-Agreements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mitigation Agreements</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mitigation Agreements...</span></span></span>

</div>

The result is an unusual organisational model in which legal ownership and operational authority become partially separated.

## How mitigation structures preserve classified access

Rather than applying a single solution, US industrial security rules provide several mitigation mechanisms matched to the degree of foreign influence.

### Board resolutions and Security Control Agreements

Where foreign ownership is relatively limited, a Board Resolution or a Security Control Agreement (SCA) may be sufficient.

Under these arrangements, foreign investors may retain representation at board level, but security controls define what classified information may be shared and how security responsibilities are exercised. DCSA notes that an SCA generally does not impose additional access restrictions on classified contracts beyond the agreed governance measures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Entity-Vetting-Facility-Clearances-FOCI/Foreign-Ownership-Control-or-Influence/Mitigation-Agreements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mitigation Agreements</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mitigation Agreements...</span></span></span>

### Special Security Agreements

When a company is effectively owned or controlled by a foreign entity, a **Special Security Agreement (SSA)** imposes much stronger internal separation.

An SSA typically requires:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* cleared US citizen officers and directors to oversee classified matters;
* a Government Security Committee responsible for classified and export-controlled activities;
* institutional security procedures independent of the foreign shareholder; and
* management positions requiring security clearances to be filled by eligible US citizens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Entity-Vetting-Facility-Clearances-FOCI/Foreign-Ownership-Control-or-Influence/Mitigation-Agreements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mitigation Agreements</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mitigation Agreements...</span></span></span>

</div>

Perhaps the most important practical consequence is that access to especially sensitive information—including Top Secret, Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI), Special Access Program information, Communications Security (COMSEC) and certain nuclear information—may additionally require a **National Interest Determination (NID)** before contracts can proceed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Entity-Vetting-Facility-Clearances-FOCI/Foreign-Ownership-Control-or-Influence/Mitigation-Agreements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mitigation Agreements</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mitigation Agreements...</span></span></span>

### Proxy Agreements and Voting Trusts

For the highest levels of mitigation, Proxy Agreements and Voting [Trust]({{ 'trust/' | relative_url }}) Agreements transfer effective corporate voting authority to approved, cleared US citizens acting independently of the foreign owner.

Although the foreign shareholder retains an economic interest, these proxy holders or trustees exercise management authority needed to protect classified work. DCSA requires them to be US citizens, resident in the United States, security-cleared and independent of both the foreign owner and the company they supervise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Entity-Vetting-Facility-Clearances-FOCI/Foreign-Ownership-Control-or-Influence/Mitigation-Agreements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mitigation Agreements</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mitigation Agreements...</span></span></span>

This creates one of the most striking examples of compartmentation inside industry: the legal owner deliberately relinquishes day-to-day control over its own classified subsidiary.

## Why Special Access Programs create additional barriers

For ordinary classified contracts, mitigation measures may be sufficient to allow participation once security requirements are satisfied.

Special Access Programs frequently demand more.

Under an SSA, access to "proscribed information"—including SAP information—can require a favourable National Interest Determination from the relevant government contracting authority before the information may be released to the contractor. This review asks not simply whether the company is cleared, but whether sharing exceptionally sensitive information with that particular ownership structure serves the national security interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/FOCI/When/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence...</span></span></span>

Consequently, two cleared contractors may have very different practical access:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* both may possess facility clearances;
* both may employ properly cleared engineers;
* yet only one may receive the most compartmented programme because its ownership structure creates fewer security concerns.

</div>

Ownership therefore becomes part of programme eligibility rather than merely a corporate governance issue.

## Operational separation inside a single corporation

FOCI mitigation affects everyday engineering and programme management, not only boardroom governance.

Depending on the mitigation plan, classified operations may include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* physically separated facilities;
* dedicated information systems;
* restricted reporting channels;
* security-approved management appointments;
* separate oversight committees; and
* carefully controlled interaction with foreign affiliates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/nisp-implementing-directive-html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nisp implementing directive html</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives32 CFR Part 2004 National Industrial Security Directive &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

</div>

Recent amendments to industrial security regulations also recognise the importance of physical separation. When a cleared contractor is located alongside a foreign parent or affiliate, DCSA may require a facilities location plan because co-location can undermine the effectiveness of FOCI mitigation. A cleared US entity generally is not expected to occupy the same location as its foreign parent where such proximity would weaken security controls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thefederalregister.org/documents/2023-27171/national-industrial-security-program-operating-manual-nispom-amendment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thefederalregister.org">[thefederalregister.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thefederalregister.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">national industrial security program operating manual nispom amendment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual (NISPOM); Amendment &#124; 88 FR 86288...</span></span></span>

These measures reduce opportunities for informal influence that would otherwise arise through shared offices, shared management systems or routine executive oversight.

## What this means for compartmented engineering teams

For engineers, ownership restrictions usually appear indirectly rather than as explicit policy.

A programme team may discover that:

* certain executives cannot attend technical reviews;
* funding decisions require specialised security procedures;
* foreign parent-company specialists cannot assist with particular problems despite possessing relevant expertise;
* cross-company collaboration requires additional [approval]({{ 'approval/' | relative_url }}) or cannot occur at all.

This can produce genuine operational costs. Engineers may lose access to technical experts elsewhere in the corporate group, duplicate capabilities already existing in affiliated companies or work through carefully sanitised interfaces rather than direct collaboration. These inefficiencies are accepted because the governing assumption is that preventing inappropriate influence over classified work outweighs the organisational advantages of unrestricted corporate integration.

For [black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}) and other highly compartmented programmes, this creates an unusual reality: the company performing the work may not be fully transparent even to its own owners. Security compartmentation extends beyond laboratories and engineering [teams]({{ 'teams/' | relative_url }}) into corporate governance itself, ensuring that control of the most sensitive contracts remains aligned with national security requirements rather than ordinary shareholder authority.

        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
