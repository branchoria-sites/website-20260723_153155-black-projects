## Introduction

The destruction of both Have Blue demonstrators might appear, at first glance, to be evidence that the programme had failed. In reality, the opposite was true. Have Blue existed to answer a narrow but revolutionary question: could an aircraft whose shape was optimised for an exceptionally small [radar signature]({{ 'radar-signature/' | relative_url }}) also be built, flown and measured in real flight? By the time the second prototype was lost in 1979, that question had already been answered. The crashes resulted from operational and mechanical failures rather than from the central stealth concept, and the programme had already generated the flight, radar and handling data needed to justify development of the [F-117]({{ 'f-117/' | relative_url }}) Nighthawk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/about/innovation-timeline/have-blue-stealth-technology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Have Blue and stealth technology &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Have Blue and stealth technology &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>

## What Happened to HB1001 After Its Landing-Gear Damage

The first demonstrator, HB1001, made its maiden flight on 1 December 1977 and completed dozens of successful test sorties. During those flights, engineers steadily refined the aircraft's fly-by-wire control system while confirming that its unusual faceted shape behaved broadly as predicted by both aerodynamic and radar models. The programme's primary objective—demonstrating that a practical low-observable aircraft could fly—was therefore already well advanced before the accident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

The accident occurred on 4 May 1978 during the aircraft's 36th flight. As test pilot Bill Park touched down, the aircraft pitched upward, resulting in an unusually hard landing. Although he initiated a go-around, the impact had damaged the landing gear, leaving it jammed in a partially retracted position. Multiple attempts to lower the gear failed.

With fuel steadily running out, Park climbed to a safe altitude and ejected. He survived, although he suffered injuries, while HB1001 crashed near Groom Lake and was destroyed. Subsequent accounts consistently describe the initiating problem as damage caused by the hard landing rather than any failure of the aircraft's low-observable design or radar-related features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

The timing mattered. By the time HB1001 was lost:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* The aircraft had accumulated enough flying time to validate key stability and control assumptions.
* Engineers had gathered extensive radar-signature measurements comparing predicted and actual performance.
* Lessons from the first aircraft had already been incorporated into construction of the second demonstrator.

</div>

As a result, the loss represented the destruction of valuable hardware, but not the loss of the programme's central technical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

## How Engine Failure and Hydraulic Loss Destroyed HB1002

The second aircraft, HB1002, incorporated improvements identified during HB1001's testing. Unlike the first demonstrator, it omitted some early flight-test equipment and was configured more directly for radar cross-section evaluation, including more complete application of radar-absorbent materials. It first flew in July 1978 under Air Force test pilot Ken Dyson.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

HB1002 completed more than fifty flights before its accident on 11 July 1979. Unlike the first crash, the initiating event was traced to a mechanical failure. An exhaust clamp loosened, allowing extremely hot engine gases to heat the adjacent engine compartment. The heat damaged nearby hydraulic lines, causing hydraulic pressure to collapse. Without hydraulic control authority, the aircraft entered severe pitch oscillations that became unrecoverable.

Dyson successfully ejected before impact, and the aircraft was destroyed. Investigators concluded that the sequence began with engine-compartment hardware failure rather than any inherent flaw in the aircraft's stealth shaping or the mathematical principles behind its low radar signature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

The distinction is important because the accident occurred after a lengthy and productive flight-test campaign. HB1002 had already contributed additional aerodynamic data, handling evaluations and radar measurements that complemented the earlier results from HB1001.

## Why Demonstrator Loss Differed from Concept Failure

Experimental aircraft frequently have short operational lives because they are built to answer specific engineering questions rather than to achieve long-term service. Have Blue illustrates this distinction especially clearly.

The programme's objectives were deliberately limited:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Verify that computer predictions of radar cross-section matched measurements on a full-scale flying aircraft.
* Demonstrate that an aircraft shaped primarily for radar avoidance could still be flown safely using digital flight controls.
* Identify practical engineering issues that would have to be solved before building an operational combat aircraft.

</div>

Those objectives had largely been achieved before either crash. The accidents therefore destroyed test vehicles without invalidating the evidence already collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

Equally significant, the flight-test programme revealed problems that could be corrected in the next design rather than fundamental reasons to abandon stealth. Engineers learned valuable lessons about centre-of-gravity management, pilot visibility, structural details, systems integration and maintainability. These findings influenced the redesign that became the Senior Trend programme and ultimately the F-117, whose wings, fuselage proportions and tail arrangement differed in several important respects from the demonstrators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Have_Blue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Have Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Have Blue</span></span></span>

From a programme-management perspective, this represented exactly what a technology demonstrator was intended to accomplish: expose risks while they remained affordable to fix.

## Why the Air Force Continued With Stealth

Perhaps the strongest evidence that the crashes did not undermine confidence in stealth is the programme timeline itself.

Even before flight testing had concluded, the Air Force had already begun planning an operational aircraft based on Have Blue's results. The Senior Trend programme, which produced the F-117 Nighthawk, moved forward because decision-makers believed the essential scientific question had been answered: low-observable shaping worked outside the laboratory. Later refinements focused on making the aircraft larger, more practical, more reliable and combat-capable rather than reconsidering whether stealth itself was viable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/history/f-117.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lockheedmartin.com">[Lockheed Martin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lockheedmartin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lockheed Martin F-117 Nighthawk &#124; Lockheed Martin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed Martin F-117 Nighthawk &#124; Lockheed Martin</span></span></span>

DARPA likewise describes Have Blue as the first practical demonstration that led directly to operational stealth aircraft, with subsequent programmes including the F-117 and later Tacit Blue building upon the validated principles rather than replacing them. The successful transition from two destroyed prototypes to multiple generations of operational stealth aircraft illustrates that experimental losses, while costly, need not overturn a programme whose underlying technical hypothesis has already been proven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/about/innovation-timeline/have-blue-stealth-technology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Have Blue and stealth technology &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Have Blue and stealth technology &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>

    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
