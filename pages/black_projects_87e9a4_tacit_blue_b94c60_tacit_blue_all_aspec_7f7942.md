## Introduction

Tacit Blue needed a different kind of stealth from earlier low-observable aircraft because its mission was fundamentally different. Rather than making a fast dash to a target, it was intended to orbit near the forward edge of a battlefield while using an advanced side-looking radar to track moving armoured forces. That exposed it to radar illumination from many directions over an extended period. As the aircraft manoeuvred, hostile radars could observe its nose, sides, rear and every angle in between. For that reason, Tacit Blue was designed around the idea of a consistently low [radar signature]({{ 'radar-signature/' | relative_url }}) across a wide range of viewing aspects—an approach often described as **all-aspect stealth**. The programme demonstrated that a surveillance aircraft could remain difficult to detect even while carrying and operating powerful sensors, and those lessons became one of Northrop's most important technological contributions to the [B-2]({{ 'b-2/' | relative_url }}) programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/195769/northrop-tacit-blue/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.af.mil">[af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

## The risks of orbiting near air defences

A conventional strike aircraft can often minimise exposure by flying a carefully planned route, presenting its most favourable aspect to known radar sites for much of the mission. Tacit Blue's concept of operations offered no such luxury.

Its Battlefield Surveillance Aircraft-Experimental (BSAX) mission required it to remain airborne for extended periods while repeatedly changing heading as it observed the battlefield. Instead of passing quickly through defended [airspace]({{ 'airspace/' | relative_url }}), it was expected to loiter close enough to detect and track enemy formations continuously. Every turn altered the aircraft's orientation relative to ground-based search radars, fire-control radars and other sensors. An aircraft with excellent frontal stealth but poor side or rear characteristics would eventually reveal itself simply through normal manoeuvring.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/news/features/stealth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>

The surveillance mission also imposed a second challenge. Tacit Blue carried an advanced side-looking radar intended to detect moving ground targets. Normally, transmitting radar energy risks revealing an aircraft's position. The programme therefore explored not only low radar cross-section shaping but also techniques allowing the aircraft's radar and associated data links to operate without readily exposing the aircraft itself. The objective was survivability during persistent observation rather than a single penetration of enemy airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/about/innovation-timeline/have-blue-stealth-technology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Have Blue and stealth technology &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Have Blue and stealth technology &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>

## Front, side and rear signature control

Designing for all-aspect stealth meant treating the aircraft as a complete three-dimensional object rather than optimising only its forward view.

Several features worked together.

* **Curved external shaping.** Unlike the sharply faceted [F-117]({{ 'f-117/' | relative_url }}), Tacit Blue demonstrated that carefully calculated curved surfaces could scatter radar energy away from hostile receivers over a broad range of viewing angles. This represented a major advance in low-observable design rather than simply a different appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/news/features/stealth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>
* **Shielded engine installation.** Engine compressor faces are among the strongest radar reflectors on an aircraft. Tacit Blue's upper-mounted inlet and integrated engine arrangement reduced direct radar visibility from below and from many forward-looking geometries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/195769/northrop-tacit-blue/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.af.mil">[Air Force Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>
* **Integrated airframe design.** The aircraft was shaped around its surveillance radar instead of adding the radar to an existing aircraft. This reduced protrusions and avoided many of the signature penalties associated with externally mounted sensors or pods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/news/features/stealth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>
* **Infrared management.** Although radar signature dominated the programme, the aircraft also incorporated measures to reduce engine heat emissions, lowering its detectability by infrared sensors as well as radar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/195769/northrop-tacit-blue/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.af.mil">[Air Force Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

These features mattered collectively because radar reflections vary dramatically with viewing angle. A design that suppresses reflections only from directly ahead may still produce large returns when viewed from the beam or rear. Tacit Blue sought to reduce those large "hot spots" so that its signature remained comparatively low throughout a typical surveillance orbit rather than only during one carefully planned approach.

## Why surveillance demanded more than forward stealth

The distinction between Tacit Blue and aircraft such as the F-117 is best understood through their operational geometry rather than their appearance.

A strike aircraft attempts to minimise the time hostile defences have to observe it. Even if its radar signature increases briefly during a turn, that exposure may last only seconds before the aircraft exits the threat area.

Tacit Blue's intended mission reversed that logic. Every additional minute spent watching enemy formations increased the opportunity for opposing radar systems to illuminate the aircraft from different bearings. The aircraft therefore had to assume that hostile sensors would eventually observe almost every side of the airframe.

This made consistent signature control more valuable than achieving an exceptionally low radar return from only one preferred direction. The surveillance mission effectively turned aircraft manoeuvring into part of the stealth problem. Maintaining low observability while changing heading, bank angle and position became essential for preserving the aircraft's ability to continue collecting intelligence without interruption.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/195769/northrop-tacit-blue/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.af.mil">[Air Force Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

## Why the challenge mattered to the B-2

Tacit Blue was not a prototype B-2, but it reduced technical uncertainty in exactly the area Northrop would later exploit.

The programme showed that smooth, continuously curved surfaces could achieve low observability without relying exclusively on the faceted geometry made famous by Have Blue and the F-117. That finding was especially important because a large [flying-wing]({{ 'flying-wing/' | relative_url }}) bomber could not simply be designed as an enlarged faceted aircraft. The aerodynamic penalties, structural complexity and operational requirements were different. Tacit Blue demonstrated that curved shaping could produce low radar signatures while also offering aerodynamic benefits such as reduced drag and greater endurance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.darpa.mil/news/features/stealth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: darpa.mil">[darpa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">darpa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stealth aircraft &#124; DARPA</span></span></span>

Equally significant was the validation of the all-aspect philosophy itself. Northrop's later B-2 was intended to penetrate sophisticated integrated [air-defence networks]({{ 'air-defence-disruption/' | relative_url }}) where threats could appear from multiple directions during long missions. Although the bomber's role differed from Tacit Blue's battlefield surveillance mission, both aircraft depended on avoiding detection throughout changing geometries rather than during a single high-speed attack run. Tacit Blue therefore provided confidence that all-aspect signature reduction was achievable in practice, not merely in theoretical calculations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.northropgrumman.com/what-we-do/aircraft/legendary-stealth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: northropgrumman.com">[northropgrumman.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">northropgrumman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Northrop Grumman Legendary Stealth &#124; Northrop Grumman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Northrop Grumman Legendary Stealth &#124; Northrop Grumman</span></span></span>

The lasting contribution of Tacit Blue was not simply proving that [curved stealth]({{ 'curved-stealth/' | relative_url }}) was possible. It demonstrated that persistent airborne surveillance demanded stealth from every practical viewing angle, and that achieving such performance required integrating sensors, airframe, propulsion and flight control into a single low-observable system. Those engineering principles became an important stepping stone on Northrop's road to the B-2.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.northropgrumman.com/what-we-do/aircraft/legendary-stealth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: northropgrumman.com">[northropgrumman.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">northropgrumman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Northrop Grumman Legendary Stealth &#124; Northrop Grumman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Northrop Grumman Legendary Stealth &#124; Northrop Grumman</span></span></span>

      var normalized = normalize(languages[i]);

        break;

      if (!languageMarket && localeMarketMap[languageOnly]) languageMarket = localeMarketMap[languageOnly];

    var matches = localeMatches();

    if (!tz) return '';
    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }

    available = Array.isArray(available) ? available : [];
    options = options || {};

      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;

    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }

    inferMarket: inferMarket,
    inferFromLocale: inferFromLocale,
    inferFromTimezone: inferFromTimezone
  };
