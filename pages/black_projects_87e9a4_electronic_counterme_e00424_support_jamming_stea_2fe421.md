## Introduction

Stealth aircraft reduce the probability of detection, but they do not make an air-defence system disappear. For high-risk penetration missions, especially those associated with advanced or classified aircraft programmes, survivability usually depends on a coordinated force package rather than a single aircraft acting alone. One of the most important supporting elements is the airborne electronic attack platform, commonly called a support jammer. Operating outside, or at the edge of, heavily defended [airspace]({{ 'airspace/' | relative_url }}), these aircraft disrupt radar, communications and command networks so that the penetrating aircraft can minimise its own radio-frequency emissions and concentrate on reaching its objective. This layered approach has been a recurring feature of US and allied electronic warfare doctrine for decades and remains relevant even as stealth technology has advanced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/files.cnas.org/documents/CNAS_ElectronicWarfare_DavisBarnoBensahel.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com">[amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CNAS Electronic Warfare Davis Barno Bensahel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CNAS Electronic Warfare Davis Barno Bensahel</span></span></span>

## What support jammers attack from outside defended airspace

Support jammers are designed to shape the electromagnetic environment before and during a strike. Unlike a stealth aircraft, whose primary defence is avoiding detection, a dedicated electronic attack aircraft deliberately radiates powerful signals to interfere with hostile sensors and communications.

Traditional stand-off jamming is conducted from beyond the engagement range of surface-to-air missile systems. The supporting aircraft remains outside the most dangerous threat envelope while transmitting enough energy to degrade surveillance radars, fire-control radars, communications links or data networks that coordinate an integrated air-defence system. Because these platforms are purpose-built for electronic attack, they can carry larger antennas, higher-power transmitters and specialist operators than a penetrating strike aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c1/General_use_of_UAS_in_EW_environment-EW_concepts_and_tactics_for_single_or_multiple_UAS_over_the_net-centric_battlefield_%28IA_generaluseofuasi109454512%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsGeneral use of UAS in EW environment--EW concepts and tactics for single or multiple UAS over the net-centric battlefiel...</span></span></span>

Modern doctrine distinguishes several related roles:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Stand-off jamming:** Wide-area disruption from outside defended airspace.
* **Escort jamming:** Electronic attack that accompanies strike aircraft closer to the threat.
* **Stand-in jamming:** Smaller expendable or highly survivable systems that move much nearer hostile radars to increase jamming effectiveness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-12-175.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">United States Government Accountability Office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">United States Government Accountability OfficeDecember 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

## How external disruption complements onboard self-protection

A common misconception is that a stealth aircraft should simply rely on its low-observable design. In reality, reducing radar reflections is only one part of surviving against a modern integrated air-defence system.

External support jammers complement onboard defensive systems in several important ways.

First, they reduce the need for the penetrating aircraft to transmit. Active electronic emissions—including high-power jamming—can themselves become detectable. If a dedicated support platform performs much of the electronic attack, the stealth aircraft can remain comparatively quiet while relying on passive sensors and carefully managed communications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/files.cnas.org/documents/CNAS_ElectronicWarfare_DavisBarnoBensahel.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com">[Amazon Web Services, Inc.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CNAS Electronic Warfare Davis Barno Bensahel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CNAS Electronic Warfare Davis Barno Bensahel</span></span></span>

Second, support jammers attack parts of the defensive network that the strike aircraft may never directly encounter. Long-range surveillance radars, communications relays and command networks often sit well away from the intended target. Disrupting these supporting systems can delay detection, reduce tracking quality or complicate coordination between different air-defence elements. Rather than defeating every radar individually, the objective is often to reduce the effectiveness of the entire defensive network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-and-electromagnetic-activities-jdn-118" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Withdrawn] Cyber and Electromagnetic Activities (JDN 1/18</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">21, 2018...</span></span></span>

Third, specialised electronic attack aircraft possess greater endurance and capacity. Because they are optimised for electronic warfare rather than strike, they can devote more onboard power, cooling and crew attention to complex electronic attack tasks than a multirole stealth aircraft typically can. The planned evolution from the EA-6B Prowler to the EA-18G Growler reflected this emphasis on integrating advanced receivers, powerful jamming systems and networked targeting support alongside strike aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monographs/2005/RAND_MG404.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Corporation This PDF document was made available</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RAND CorporationThis PDF document was made available...</span></span></span>

## Why survivability depends on a coordinated force package

Modern air operations are increasingly organised around mutually supporting capabilities rather than individual aircraft performance.

A representative penetration package may combine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Low-observable strike aircraft approaching the target with carefully controlled emissions.
* Dedicated electronic attack aircraft disrupting radar and communications.
* Intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance assets updating the electronic picture.
* Suppression of enemy air defences using anti-radiation weapons against emitting radars.
* Decoys and expendable jammers creating additional false targets and electronic clutter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/files.cnas.org/documents/CNAS_ElectronicWarfare_DavisBarnoBensahel.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com">[amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CNAS Electronic Warfare Davis Barno Bensahel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CNAS Electronic Warfare Davis Barno Bensahel</span></span></span>

</div>

Each element reduces pressure on the others. The stealth platform does not need to reveal itself through extensive electronic transmissions, while the support jammer benefits from the strike aircraft's ability to penetrate closer to defended objectives.

This approach also provides resilience. If one radar remains operational or one defensive sector proves more capable than expected, multiple supporting systems can compensate instead of depending on a single aircraft's onboard countermeasures.

## Stand-in systems are extending the concept

Recent developments have expanded support jamming beyond large electronic warfare aircraft.

One notable example is the Miniature Air-Launched Decoy-Jammer (MALD-J), an expendable stand-in jammer. Rather than remaining outside defended airspace, MALD-J flies much closer to hostile radar systems while transmitting deceptive or jamming signals. Because it approaches the radar directly, it can achieve stronger local effects than distant stand-off platforms despite being much smaller. It may also imitate the signatures and flight profiles of manned aircraft, forcing defenders to waste attention and resources on false targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/air/mald-decoy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MALD Decoy &#124; Raytheon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MALD Decoy &#124; Raytheon...</span></span></span>

This evolution illustrates an important trend: support jamming increasingly combines manned aircraft, expendable decoys and networked electronic attack rather than relying on a single specialised platform.

## What public evidence suggests about classified programmes

Details of electronic warfare support for genuinely classified aircraft remain highly restricted. Public sources rarely identify the exact tactics, waveforms or force compositions associated with black programmes.

Nevertheless, official doctrine, procurement history and [declassified]({{ 'declassified/' | relative_url }}) capability descriptions consistently point towards the same operational principle: stealth aircraft are intended to operate within a broader electronic warfare architecture rather than independently. The transition from the EA-6B to the EA-18G, continuing investment in next-generation airborne jammers, and development of stand-in systems such as MALD-J all indicate that planners expect future penetration missions to combine low observability with coordinated electronic attack instead of treating them as competing alternatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monographs/2005/RAND_MG404.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[rand.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Corporation This PDF document was made available</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RAND CorporationThis PDF document was made available...</span></span></span>
