## Introduction

Within the system of US Special Access Programs (SAPs), a **waived SAP** is not a higher classification than other SAPs. Instead, it is an exceptionally restricted category of **unacknowledged** SAP in which the normal methods of informing Congress are limited because the Secretary of Defense has determined that using standard reporting procedures would itself harm national security. Even so, a waived SAP is **not exempt from congressional oversight**. The legal framework narrows who receives information and how it is handled, rather than eliminating oversight altogether. This distinction is important because public discussions often portray waived SAPs as programmes hidden from Congress entirely, whereas the governing statutes and Department of Defense (DoD) policy describe a much narrower reporting channel instead.

## What makes a waived SAP different?

Most Special Access Programs already impose controls beyond ordinary classified information through compartmented access, specialised facilities and strict "[need to know]({{ 'need-to-know/' | relative_url }})" rules. A waived SAP adds another layer by limiting the normal congressional reporting process.

Current DoD policy states that the Secretary of Defense may designate **selected unacknowledged SAPs** as waived when using standard congressional reporting procedures would adversely affect national security. The policy explicitly ties this authority to the congressional oversight provisions in federal law and emphasises that **waived SAPs remain subject to congressional oversight**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS Enterprise Services Division]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS Enterprise Services DivisionDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; Sept...</span></span></span>

Several features distinguish waived SAPs from ordinary SAPs:

The result is a reporting mechanism designed for programmes considered so sensitive that even the routine distribution of programme information within Congress is judged to increase operational risk.

## Why normal reporting can be limited

Congress created annual reporting requirements for DoD SAPs to prevent highly classified programmes from operating indefinitely without legislative awareness. Under 10 U.S.C. §119, the Secretary of Defense must normally provide [annual reports]({{ 'annual-reports/' | relative_url }}) describing SAP funding and programme information to the congressional defence committees. The same statute, however, recognises that exceptional cases may require narrower reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A10+section%3A119+edition%3Aprelim%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Code</span></span></span>

The law therefore permits the Secretary to waive specific reporting requirements **on a case-by-case basis** if including particular information in an ordinary report would adversely affect national security. This authority is deliberately framed as an exception rather than the default.

Importantly, the statute does not authorise a blanket disappearance of oversight. If information is withheld from the standard report, the Secretary must still provide the relevant information and the justification for using the waiver through the alternative reporting mechanism established by law.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A10+section%3A119+edition%3Aprelim%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Code</span></span></span>

This structure reflects a governance compromise. Congress accepted that a small number of programmes might require extraordinary secrecy, while simultaneously requiring that the executive branch remain accountable through carefully restricted disclosure.

## What oversight still remains on paper?

The common misconception is that a waived SAP can be hidden from Congress altogether. The statutory framework says otherwise.

### Restricted recipients rather than no recipients

Current DoD procedures specify that members of the defence and intelligence committees generally receive access to SAPs within their oversight jurisdiction. Waived programmes are treated differently.

For waived DoD SAPs, routine access is limited to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* the committee chair;
* the ranking minority member;
* the staff director; and
* one designated security manager.

</div>

Any broader access requires [approval]({{ 'approval/' | relative_url }}) by the Secretary or Deputy Secretary of Defense.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf?ver=2020-02-04-142758-247" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS Enterprise Services Division]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS Enterprise Services DivisionDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; Sept...</span></span></span>

This significantly reduces the number of people who receive programme information while preserving an institutional oversight channel.

### Special handling of congressional material

DoD policy also imposes unusual controls on congressional handling of waived SAP information. SAP material provided to Congress is managed through the DoD SAP Central Office (SAPCO), and the department controls delivery, receipt and handling procedures. Waived programme material receives tighter custody controls than ordinary SAP reporting because limiting document circulation is part of the security rationale behind the waiver.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf?ver=2020-02-04-142758-247" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS Enterprise Services Division]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS Enterprise Services DivisionDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; Sept...</span></span></span>

### Annual reporting does not disappear

Federal law still requires annual SAP reporting. The waiver provision applies only to specified information whose inclusion in ordinary reports would damage national security.

When the Secretary exercises waiver authority, the statute requires that both:

* the withheld programme information; and
* the justification for the waiver

be provided through the restricted congressional reporting process described in law.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A10+section%3A119+edition%3Aprelim%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Code</span></span></span>

The legal theory is therefore one of **selective disclosure**, not complete executive autonomy.

## The balance between secrecy and accountability

Waived SAPs illustrate a recurring tension in national security governance. The executive branch argues that some programmes—particularly those involving exceptionally sensitive intelligence methods, advanced military capabilities or highly vulnerable technologies—cannot tolerate the wider distribution associated with ordinary reporting.

Congress, however, has consistently insisted that extraordinary secrecy should not eliminate legislative oversight. That is why the statutory framework combines three elements:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* authority to limit standard reporting when national security requires it;
* mandatory case-by-case justification for doing so; and
* continued reporting to designated congressional leadership rather than complete non-disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A10+section%3A119+edition%3Aprelim%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Code</span></span></span>

</div>

This arrangement attempts to reconcile two competing constitutional interests: protecting highly sensitive defence capabilities while preserving Congress's authority over appropriations, oversight and national defence.

## Common misunderstandings

Public discussion of [black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}) frequently exaggerates what a waived SAP designation means.

**Myth: A waived SAP is completely outside congressional oversight.**

The governing statutes and DoD policy explicitly state otherwise. Waived programmes continue to be subject to congressional oversight through restricted reporting channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS Enterprise Services Division]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS Enterprise Services DivisionDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; Sept...</span></span></span>

**Myth: "Waived" means the programme itself is exempt from law.**

The waiver concerns reporting procedures, not exemption from legal authorities governing classification, appropriations or executive [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}). The designation changes who receives information and how it is transmitted.

**Myth: Every black project is a waived SAP.**

Only selected unacknowledged SAPs qualify, and the designation requires a determination that ordinary reporting would itself harm national security. Most SAPs are not waived and follow the normal congressional reporting framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS Enterprise Services Division]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS Enterprise Services DivisionDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; Sept...</span></span></span>

## Why waived SAPs matter in discussions of black projects

Waived SAPs occupy the narrowest reporting lane within the established SAP system. They demonstrate that the US government has recognised circumstances in which even the ordinary oversight process may expose exceptionally sensitive capabilities. At the same time, the governing statutes reject the idea that such programmes should operate without legislative knowledge.

In practice, the waiver mechanism narrows the flow of information rather than stopping it. Oversight is concentrated among a very small number of congressional leaders and authorised staff, reflecting an effort to preserve constitutional accountability while minimising the security risks associated with wider disclosure.
