## Introduction

Directed-energy weapons (DEWs) are often presented as the next generation of military technology because they can engage targets at the speed of light and, in principle, fire repeatedly without consuming conventional ammunition. Live-fire demonstrations have shown high-energy lasers destroying drones and high-power microwave systems disrupting electronics, proving that the underlying physics works. Yet many prototypes remain demonstrators rather than widely fielded weapons. The gap is not usually caused by a single scientific obstacle. Instead, it reflects the difficulty of turning a successful range test into a dependable military capability that can survive harsh environments, integrate with existing forces, justify its cost and fit within long-term procurement programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 105868</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons: DOD Should Focus on Transition Planning &#124; U.S. GAOApril 17, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 2023</span></span></span>

Within the wider history of classified and advanced weapons research, directed-energy programmes illustrate a recurring pattern: technically impressive prototypes can generate genuine breakthroughs while still falling short of operational deployment because engineering, logistics, doctrine and acquisition prove harder than laboratory demonstrations.

## What live-fire demonstrations actually prove

Successful demonstrations are important milestones, but they answer only a limited question: can a prototype damage or disable a target under carefully managed conditions?

Modern trials have repeatedly shown that lasers can destroy small unmanned aircraft and that microwave systems can interfere with groups of electronic targets. Programmes such as the US Army's Directed Energy Maneuver Short-Range Air Defense (DE M-SHORAD), the Navy's Laser Weapon System (LaWS), Britain's DragonFire and Germany's naval laser demonstrators have all demonstrated accurate tracking and successful engagements against representative targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article-amp/286677/us_army_tests_laser_weapons_aiming_at_a_future_of_energy_based_air_defense" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[army.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">US Army tests laser weapons, aiming at a future of energy-based air defense</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">US Army tests laser weapons, aiming at a future of energy-based air defenseJune 27, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2025</span></span></span>

These events prove several important capabilities:

* Precision beam control and target tracking.
* Integration between sensors, fire-control software and the weapon.
* Sufficient energy delivery to defeat specific classes of targets.
* Basic safety and reliability during controlled operation.

</div>

However, they do not automatically demonstrate that the system can:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* operate continuously for extended periods;
* withstand battlefield dust, vibration, salt spray or extreme temperatures;
* engage many different target types under unpredictable conditions;
* remain affordable to maintain over years of military service; or
* integrate smoothly into existing logistics and command structures.

</div>

In other words, destroying a drone on a test range is evidence that a technology works—not proof that it is ready to replace established air-defence systems. ## Power, cooling and environmental limits

The largest engineering challenge is rarely producing the laser beam itself. It is generating, storing and managing enough electrical power while removing the enormous waste heat created during operation.

### Electrical power becomes a platform problem

High-energy lasers are relatively efficient compared with earlier generations, but they still convert a substantial share of input electricity into heat rather than usable beam energy.

That means a mobile laser weapon requires much more than the laser module itself. It also needs:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* electrical generators or batteries;
* power-conditioning equipment;
* thermal management systems;
* cooling circuits and heat exchangers;
* structural support for all this additional hardware.

</div>

The result is increased size, weight and power demand—often summarised in defence engineering as the "SWaP" challenge (size, weight and power). Vehicles with limited electrical capacity may require extensive redesign before they can support an operational laser. Naval ships generally face fewer constraints because they possess much larger power generation and cooling reserves, which helps explain why many early operational systems have appeared at sea rather than on tactical ground vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-products/product/pdf/R/R46925" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: congress.gov">[congress.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">congress.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on congress.gov.</span></span></span>

### Heat limits sustained firing

Unlike conventional missiles, lasers are often described as having an almost unlimited magazine because they use electricity instead of stored interceptors.

In practice, heat creates another operational limit.

After repeated engagements, cooling systems may require time to dissipate accumulated heat before full-power firing can continue. Designers therefore have to balance:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* laser power;
* firing rate;
* thermal storage;
* cooling capacity; and
* available electrical generation.

</div>

This engineering trade-off becomes especially demanding for mobile platforms expected to operate for prolonged periods without extensive support infrastructure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-and-security-accelerator-dasa-open-call-for-innovation/ifa035-making-science-fiction-a-reality-future-directed-energy-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IFA035 - Making Science Fiction a Reality: Future Directed Energy Weapons - GOV.UK...</span></span></span>

### Weather remains an operational constraint

Laser energy must travel through the atmosphere before reaching its target.

Rain, fog, smoke, dust, turbulence and airborne particles scatter or absorb part of the beam, reducing effective range or increasing the time needed to inflict damage. Unlike cinematic portrayals, laser effects are not instantaneous against every target. Many engagements require maintaining precise aim on one location long enough to heat, weaken or burn through material.

Fast-moving manoeuvring targets therefore increase the difficulty considerably, particularly when atmospheric distortion is already degrading beam quality. These limitations are well recognised in defence assessments and remain important considerations when deciding which missions are suitable for laser weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R46925" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: congress.gov">[Congress.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">congress.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 11, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why acquisition programmes lose momentum

Engineering challenges explain only part of the story. Institutional factors often determine whether prototypes become enduring military capabilities.

### The "valley of death" between research and procurement

The GAO has identified a persistent gap between prototype development organisations and acquisition authorities, often called the "valley of death".

Research organisations optimise for technical innovation, while acquisition programmes must demonstrate affordability, long-term support, manufacturing readiness and military usefulness.

A successful prototype can therefore complete its experimental objectives yet still lack:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a funded production programme;
* defined operational requirements;
* industrial manufacturing capacity; or
* clear ownership by a military service.

</div>

Without these elements, promising technology can remain in prolonged experimentation despite repeated technical successes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 105868</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons: DOD Should Focus on Transition Planning &#124; U.S. GAOApril 17, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 2023</span></span></span>

### Mission requirements continue to evolve

The rapid growth of inexpensive drones has strengthened the case for directed-energy weapons because conventional missile interceptors can be disproportionately expensive against low-cost targets.

At the same time, military planners frequently raise expectations beyond the original mission.

A laser capable of defeating commercial drones may later be expected to stop:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* cruise missiles;
* rockets;
* artillery shells;
* mortar rounds; or
* large coordinated attacks.

</div>

Each increase in target complexity generally demands more power, longer engagement ranges, improved tracking and more robust thermal management. As requirements expand, programmes often require redesign rather than straightforward production.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-products/product/pdf/R/R46925" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: congress.gov">[congress.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">congress.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on congress.gov.</span></span></span>

### Integration costs exceed the weapon itself

Fielding a weapon involves much more than manufacturing emitters.

Military organisations must also provide:

* operator training;
* [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) procedures;
* spare parts;
* software updates;
* cybersecurity;
* integration with radar and command networks;
* safety certification; and
* logistics for deployed forces.

These supporting systems frequently account for a significant share of programme complexity and cost, reducing the apparent advantage of a successful prototype.

## Why some programmes continue while others stall

Recent developments suggest that directed-energy weapons are gradually moving from experimental projects towards specialised operational roles rather than replacing conventional weapons outright.

Naval platforms remain attractive because their larger power supplies simplify integration. Fixed-site defence against drones also offers favourable operating conditions compared with highly mobile frontline formations. This gradual approach reflects an important lesson from decades of advanced weapons research. Directed-energy technology has matured substantially, but operational success depends on matching the weapon to missions where its strengths—speed-of-light engagement, low cost per shot and deep magazines—outweigh persistent limitations in power generation, cooling, atmospheric performance and platform integration. Rather than representing failed science, many stalled programmes demonstrate the difference between proving that a technology can work and proving that it can work reliably, affordably and repeatedly under the demands of real military service.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 105868</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons: DOD Should Focus on Transition Planning &#124; U.S. GAOApril 17, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 2023</span></span></span>

    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
