## Introduction

[Black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}) do not normally escape government accounting systems. Instead, the identity of the programme receiving the money is often concealed from the public by how budgets are presented, classified and attributed. Public budget documents may show a broad account, a generic programme title or a classified line item without revealing which specific capability is being financed. This approach preserves formal legal appropriations while reducing the risk that an adversary could infer sensitive military or intelligence capabilities from published financial records. At the same time, the money remains subject to classified oversight by authorised officials, inspectors and selected members of Congress rather than disappearing into an unrecorded fund.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased &#124; U.S. GAODecember 21, 1992...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

Understanding this distinction is essential when examining black projects. The central question is not whether the expenditure exists on paper—it almost always does—but how the government obscures who ultimately receives the funding while maintaining [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) within classified channels.

## Where does black-project money appear on paper?

Most funding for highly classified defence programmes enters the budget through ordinary appropriations such as research and development, procurement or operations accounts. The public version of the budget may identify only a broad organisational owner or include a generic description such as "classified programme" rather than naming the actual capability. This means the appropriation is visible, but its operational destination is not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12943" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: congress.gov">[Congress.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">congress.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 14, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2025</span></span></span>

The technique relies on separating three different questions:

* **Who appropriated the money?** Congress still passes appropriations through established legal processes.
* **Which account contains it?** The funding appears within an authorised budget account, sometimes grouped with other classified activities.
* **Which programme ultimately spends it?** That information may be withheld from public documents and disclosed only through classified annexes or secure briefings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

This distinction explains why observers examining published budgets often cannot identify which visible line funds a specific aircraft, satellite or intelligence system, even though auditors and authorised oversight bodies can trace the expenditure internally.

## Obscured attribution in public budgets

The objective is usually to obscure attribution rather than conceal expenditure itself.

Public budget books frequently aggregate multiple classified activities into a single funding line or replace detailed descriptions with deliberately broad labels. Such presentation prevents readers from linking a specific appropriation to an identifiable capability. Even when the total value of classified spending is known, the allocation among individual programmes remains concealed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12943" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: congress.gov">[Congress.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">congress.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 14, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2025</span></span></span>

Several mechanisms contribute to this obscurity:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Generic programme descriptions** that reveal little beyond a funding category.
* **Aggregation** of several classified efforts into one published account.
* **Classified budget justification documents**, provided only to cleared congressional committees.
* **Redacted supporting material**, leaving only totals or high-level descriptions in public releases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

</div>

These methods reduce the intelligence value of public financial documents. If every advanced reconnaissance aircraft or satellite programme had its own descriptive budget line, foreign intelligence services could reconstruct procurement priorities simply by reading published appropriations.

## How classified oversight still follows the money

A common misconception is that hidden budget descriptions eliminate oversight. In practice, US law requires reporting of Special Access Programs (SAPs) through classified channels.

Under 10 U.S.C. §119, the Secretary of Defense must provide the congressional defence committees with annual classified reports covering special access programmes. Those reports include programme descriptions, milestones, historical costs, current spending and projected future costs. Comparable statutory reporting requirements also exist for certain intelligence-community and National Nuclear Security Administration programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[cornell.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

This creates two parallel versions of budget accountability:

Public processClassified processBroad appropriations and limited descriptionsDetailed programme identities and financial dataPublic budget booksClassified annexes and secure briefingsGeneral taxpayer transparencyCleared congressional oversightLimited operational detailFull cost, schedule and programme reporting

The arrangement reflects a governance trade-off. Legislators judged to have the necessary security clearances and committee responsibilities receive substantially more information than the public, allowing formal constitutional oversight while attempting to protect operational security.

## Why vague spending patterns attract speculation

Because the public cannot easily connect published appropriations with specific classified activities, ambiguous budget lines often become fertile ground for speculation.

Large generic accounts, redacted justification books or unexpectedly growing classified appropriations sometimes generate claims that governments are concealing entirely unreported programmes. However, the existence of vague budget language alone does not demonstrate unlawful spending. In many cases, classification reflects established security policy intended to prevent disclosure of military capabilities rather than evidence that funds have escaped legal appropriation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased &#124; U.S. GAODecember 21, 1992...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

That does not mean oversight concerns are imaginary. Government Accountability Office reviews have historically found weaknesses in the management and oversight of some Special Access Programs, including inconsistent controls and earlier concerns about highly compartmented "carve-out" arrangements that limited routine security inspection. These findings focused on improving governance and accountability within classified systems rather than suggesting that programmes existed entirely outside government accounting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased &#124; U.S. GAODecember 21, 1992...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

The recurring tension is therefore between two legitimate objectives:

* protecting genuinely sensitive national-security capabilities from [public disclosure]({{ 'public-disclosure/' | relative_url }}); and
* ensuring that secrecy does not weaken financial accountability or congressional supervision.

## The practical limits of budget secrecy

Classified budgeting is effective at concealing attribution, but it rarely makes spending completely invisible.

Major programmes still leave administrative traces. Money must pass through appropriated accounts, contracts must be awarded, personnel must be paid and financial systems must reconcile expenditures. While many of these records remain classified, they continue to exist for auditors, inspectors general and authorised oversight committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

Moreover, very large procurement programmes can become difficult to mask entirely. Industrial capacity, specialised suppliers, workforce growth and long-term appropriations may collectively reveal that substantial classified activity is occurring, even if the precise recipient remains unknown. Analysts therefore often identify trends in classified spending without being able to determine exactly which hidden capability those funds support.

The result is a budgeting system designed not to erase financial accountability, but to separate public visibility from classified knowledge. Black-project money generally appears within authorised government accounts; what remains hidden is the final attribution linking a published budget line to the specific secret programme that ultimately receives and spends the funds.

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
