## Introduction

Before a classified US Department of Defense (DoD) Special Access Program (SAP) is publicly acknowledged or information from it is officially [declassified]({{ 'declassified/' | relative_url }}), Congress is not supposed to learn about the change from a press release or public announcement. Federal law requires the Secretary of Defense to notify the relevant congressional defence committees in advance, explain why the classification is changing, and describe any planned public announcement. In most cases, that notification must arrive at least 14 days before the information is released publicly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

This advance-notice requirement is a relatively narrow but important part of congressional oversight of [black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}). It does not decide whether a programme should be declassified. Instead, it ensures that the committees responsible for overseeing highly classified defence activities have an opportunity to understand and question the decision before it becomes public.

## The 14-day report rule

The key legal requirement appears in **10 U.S.C. §119(c)**, which governs congressional oversight of DoD Special Access Programs.

* a change in the classification status of a SAP; or
* the declassification and public release of classified information about a SAP,

</div>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a description of the proposed classification change or declassification;
* the reasons for making that change; and

## What committees learn before public announcements

The advance report is not sent to every member of Congress. It goes to the statutory "defence committees", namely:

* the Senate Armed Services Committee;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/2025/07/17/intelligence-authorization-act-for-fiscal-year-2026-as-reported-on-july-17-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intelligence.senate.gov">[intelligence.senate.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intelligence.senate.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intelligence authorization act for fiscal year 2026 as reported on july</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">intelligence authorization act for fiscal year 2026 as reported on july</span></span></span> * the House Armed Services Committee;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=1999&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-1999-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[uscode.house.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* the Senate Appropriations Committee and its Defence Subcommittee; and
* the House Appropriations Committee and its Defence Subcommittee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A10+section%3A119+edition%3Aprelim%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Code</span></span></span>

The report is intended to answer practical oversight questions rather than merely announcing that information will become public. Committee members are expected to receive enough information to understand:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exactly what information is being declassified or reclassified;
* why the Department believes disclosure is appropriate;
* whether the proposed public explanation accurately reflects the programme; and
* whether any security or budgetary concerns remain unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

</div>

The process therefore treats declassification as another oversight decision rather than simply a public-relations exercise.

## Why advance notice matters

Public acknowledgement of a previously secret programme can have significant consequences even when the technology itself is no longer especially sensitive.

Official disclosure may:

* reveal years of previously hidden defence spending;
* identify [contractors]({{ 'contractors/' | relative_url }}) or facilities;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* change international perceptions of US military capabilities;
* prompt questions about earlier congressional funding decisions; or
* require changes to future procurement or operational planning.

</div>

Because of these wider consequences, Congress is expected to know why the Executive Branch believes disclosure serves the national interest before the announcement is made. The notice requirement helps preserve legislative oversight even as secrecy is reduced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

## The exception for exceptional circumstances

The 14-day period is not absolute.

The statute allows the Secretary of Defense to provide notice less than 14 days in advance if "exceptional circumstances" make compliance impossible. In that case, the report must still be submitted **before** the public announcement or classification change takes effect, and it must explain why the normal advance-notice requirement could not be met.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

Congress therefore recognised that unexpected operational, diplomatic or security developments might occasionally require faster action. However, the law still preserves the principle that the oversight committees should receive notice before, not after, [public disclosure]({{ 'public-disclosure/' | relative_url }}) whenever feasible.

## Why declassification can remain politically sensitive

Changing the classification of a black programme is often politically sensitive because it can reshape public understanding of past government decisions.

Questions may arise over:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether Congress previously received adequate information;
* how much money was spent under classified appropriations;
* whether earlier public statements were intentionally limited by security requirements; and
* whether additional classified capabilities remain protected.

</div>

For that reason, declassification decisions often involve careful coordination among defence officials, classification authorities, lawyers and congressional staff. The statutory notice requirement helps ensure that oversight committees are not surprised by major public disclosures concerning programmes they have been responsible for supervising.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

## A broader oversight pattern

The advance-notice requirement fits into a wider framework established by 10 U.S.C. §119.

The same statute requires annual reporting on SAPs, advance notification when new SAPs are created, explanations for why programmes receive special access protection, and notification when the policies governing SAP designation change. The declassification notice provision complements those reporting duties by ensuring that congressional oversight continues not only when programmes become secret, but also when they cease to be secret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>
