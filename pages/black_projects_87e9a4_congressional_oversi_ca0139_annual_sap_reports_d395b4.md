## Introduction

Annual reporting is one of the principal ways Congress oversees highly classified Department of Defense Special Access Programs (SAPs). Although these programmes may remain invisible to the public, they are not intended to operate without congressional knowledge. Under 10 U.S.C. §119, the Secretary of Defense must provide the congressional defence committees with recurring classified reports that identify what SAPs exist, how much they cost, where they stand in their development, and why new programmes require extraordinary secrecy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

The result is a system in which secrecy and oversight coexist. The public does not receive these reports, and even most Members of Congress do not routinely see them. Instead, selected committees receive detailed information through secure classified channels so that they can authorise funding, question programme managers, and judge whether exceptional secrecy remains justified.

## What 10 U.S.C. §119 requires each year

The annual reporting requirement is more demanding than a simple notification that secret programmes exist. The law specifies that, no later than 1 March each year, the Secretary of Defense must submit a report on Department of Defense SAPs to the congressional defence committees. The report accompanies the President's budget cycle and is designed to give legislators sufficient information to evaluate classified programmes alongside the rest of the defence budget.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

For each annual report, Congress is entitled to receive:

* The total amount requested in the President's budget for Department of Defense SAPs.
* For every SAP included in that budget:
a brief description of the programme;
its major development or acquisition milestones;
actual costs incurred during previous fiscal years;
estimated total programme cost;
projected spending for the current fiscal year, the requested budget year, and the following four fiscal years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

This structure gives congressional reviewers both historical and forward-looking information. Rather than seeing only a single annual funding request, committees can compare planned spending against previous expenditures and expected future commitments.

The statute also contains provisions for years in which the Department of Defense uses multi-year budgeting. In those cases, the report is adjusted so that Congress still receives comparable financial information even when a traditional annual budget request is not submitted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

## Costs, milestones and descriptions in classified form

A common misconception is that congressional oversight consists only of top-level funding numbers. The statutory reporting requirements are broader than that.

### Programme descriptions

The required "brief description" is intended to identify what a programme is designed to accomplish without making it a public document. In practice, these descriptions are delivered in classified form inside secure congressional facilities rather than published in budget books or committee reports. Members and cleared staff use them to distinguish between programmes with similar missions and to understand the operational purpose of requested funding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

### Major milestones

Reporting on major milestones allows committees to judge whether a programme is progressing as expected. Depending on the nature of the SAP, milestones may relate to technology development, testing, procurement, deployment or other measurable stages of execution. By comparing successive annual reports, congressional overseers can identify delays, schedule changes or significant restructuring.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

### Financial history and future obligations

The financial reporting requirement is particularly important because it presents more than a single appropriation request.

Congress receives:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* actual historical expenditures;
* current-year spending estimates;
* requested funding;
* projected costs over the next four fiscal years; and
* an estimate of total programme cost.

</div>

This enables defence committees to assess whether programmes are expanding unexpectedly, remaining within anticipated budgets or developing long-term financial commitments that may affect future appropriations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

## Annual reports are only one part of the oversight cycle

The annual SAP report operates alongside several additional reporting obligations rather than replacing them.

Separate annual reporting is required for newly designated SAPs. By 1 February, the Secretary of Defense must notify the defence committees of every new SAP designation and explain why special access protection is necessary. That submission also includes the current estimated programme cost and identifies existing programmes or technologies with comparable missions or capabilities, helping Congress evaluate whether exceptional secrecy is warranted or whether similar work already exists elsewhere in the Department.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

The law also requires advance notification when the Department plans to change the classification status of a SAP or publicly disclose classified information about one. This ensures that Congress is informed before significant changes in public visibility occur rather than learning about them only after announcements have been made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

Together, these recurring reports give congressional committees a continuing picture of programmes throughout their life cycle instead of relying solely on isolated funding decisions.

## Why annual reporting is oversight, not public disclosure

The statutory reporting system illustrates an important distinction in American national security law: information can remain secret from the public while still being disclosed to authorised congressional overseers.

The annual SAP reports are classified documents. They are not released under the ordinary federal budget process, and they do not appear in public appropriations documents. Instead, they are transmitted through secure classified channels to the congressional defence committees, whose members and appropriately cleared staff review them in secure facilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

This arrangement serves two governance objectives simultaneously:

* protecting operational details that could damage national security if publicly released; and
* preserving Congress's constitutional role in authorising expenditures and overseeing executive branch activities.

Because committees receive programme descriptions, cost histories and projected spending, oversight extends beyond approving a single classified budget line. Legislators have a statutory basis for asking whether programmes remain necessary, whether costs are justified, and whether promised milestones are being achieved.

The system is therefore designed to prevent highly sensitive defence programmes from existing entirely outside congressional review, even though their existence and details may remain unknown to the wider public.

## What the reports do not guarantee

Receiving annual reports does not necessarily mean that every Member of Congress gains full visibility into every classified programme. The statutory reports are directed to the congressional defence committees rather than to Congress as a whole, and access remains governed by classification rules and committee responsibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

Nor do the reporting requirements eliminate debates about whether congressional oversight is always complete in practice. Questions have periodically arisen over exceptionally sensitive programmes, compartmented access arrangements and the effectiveness of legislative oversight. Those debates concern how oversight is implemented, however, rather than what the reporting statute itself requires.

The statutory framework is clear on one central point: Department of Defense SAPs are intended to be subject to recurring classified reporting that gives designated congressional committees information on programme purpose, milestones and costs, even when those same programmes remain entirely absent from the public record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>
