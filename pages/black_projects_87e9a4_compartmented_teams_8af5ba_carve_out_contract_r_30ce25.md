## Introduction

Carve-out contracts are a specialised form of contract used within certain highly classified United States Special Access Programs (SAPs). Their defining feature is not simply that the work is secret, but that responsibility for industrial security oversight is removed from the normal government security organisation and assigned directly to the sponsoring programme office or another designated government component. This arrangement is intended for the rare cases where even knowledge that a contract exists—or that a contractor is associated with a particular programme—is itself classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/32/117.6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute32 CFR § 117.6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Responsibilities. &#124; Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (e-CFR) &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

Within the broader world of [black projects]({{ 'black-projects/' | relative_url }}) and compartmented engineering teams, carve-outs offer an important security advantage: they sharply reduce the number of organisations aware of sensitive work. At the same time, they create governance challenges because many of the independent inspection and administrative functions that normally provide external scrutiny become concentrated inside the programme's own security structure. Government reviews have repeatedly concluded that this concentration can weaken [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) unless compensating oversight mechanisms are rigorously maintained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/ggd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ggd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) Contracts &#124; U.S. GAOFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

## Why programmes leave standard security oversight

The ordinary National Industrial Security Program relies on a central government security authority to inspect cleared [contractors]({{ 'contractors/' | relative_url }}), review classified storage, monitor document control and verify compliance with security regulations. A carve-out deliberately departs from that model.

Under current Department of Defense policy, the Secretary of Defense or Deputy Secretary of Defense may authorise a carve-out when the security interests of the programme require relieving the Defence Counterintelligence and Security Agency (formerly the Defence Security Service) of its normal oversight role. The justification is generally that merely revealing the existence of the contract or its relationship to the SAP would expose protected information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/32/117.6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute32 CFR § 117.6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Responsibilities. &#124; Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (e-CFR) &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

The principal security gains include:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://govtribe.com/file/government-file/21p0000000304-dodm-5220-dot-22-volume-2-august-1-2018-dot-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govtribe.com">[govtribe.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govtribe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">21p0000000304 dodm 5220 dot 22 volume 2 august 1 2018 dot pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">21p0000000304 dodm 5220 dot 22 volume 2 august 1 2018 dot pdf</span></span></span>

* **Reduced visibility.** Fewer government offices know which companies are supporting the programme.
* **Tighter operational security.** Contractor records, security reporting and inspections are handled through the sponsoring SAP office instead of routine administrative channels.
* **Protection against inference.** Ordinary procurement records, inspection schedules or facility listings cannot as easily reveal that a particular contractor supports a sensitive capability.
* **Greater [compartmentation]({{ 'compartmentation/' | relative_url }}).** Security decisions remain inside the small group already authorised to know the programme's existence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/afmc/publication/dodm5220.22v2_afman16-1406v2_afmcsup/dodm5220.22v2_afman16-1406v2_afmcsup.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E-PublishingAdministrative Changes to AFMAN 16-1406_afmcsup, National Industrial Security Program...</span></span></span>

These benefits are aimed at protecting exceptionally sensitive capabilities rather than making procurement generally more secret. Regulations therefore require specific [approval]({{ 'approval/' | relative_url }}) rather than allowing programmes to designate carve-outs at their own discretion. Historical revisions to DoD policy also added requirements that carve-outs support approved SAPs and receive separate justification rather than being assumed automatically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/ggd-83-43.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GGD-83-43 Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) ContractsFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

## What the separate security office must do

A carve-out does not eliminate security oversight. Instead, it transfers much of that responsibility from the normal industrial security organisation to the designated government security office responsible for the SAP.

In practice, the sponsoring office assumes responsibility for functions that would otherwise be distributed among standard oversight organisations. These typically include:

* approving contractor access;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://gao.justia.com/department-of-defense/1986/8/information-security-nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.justia.com">[gao.justia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">information security nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">information security nsiad</span></span></span>

Because these duties are concentrated within the programme office, regulations emphasise formal security documentation. Earlier revisions to DoD guidance required each component to establish a single point of responsibility for SAP and carve-out administration and to prepare written security plans for individual carve-out contracts. The intent was to replace informal arrangements with documented governance despite the exceptional secrecy involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/ggd-83-43.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GGD-83-43 Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) ContractsFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

## Where reviewers found the greatest risks

The strongest public evidence regarding carve-out weaknesses comes from repeated reviews by the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO).

### Independent inspection can become weaker

A major GAO review in 1983 concluded that the growing use of carve-out contracts had created inconsistent security administration across the Department of Defense. Because inspection responsibility shifted away from the normal industrial security organisation, reviewers found uncertainty over how many carve-out contracts existed, varying security practices among sponsoring components and cases where contracts were reportedly not inspected at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/ggd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ggd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) Contracts &#124; U.S. GAOFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

The report recommended stronger central inventories, annual validation of carve-out status and broader independent inspection responsibilities to reduce the risk that programmes could operate under differing security standards. Although not all recommendations were adopted, the review established a recurring concern: exceptional secrecy should not eliminate consistent oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/ggd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ggd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) Contracts &#124; U.S. GAOFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

### Recordkeeping can suffer even with on-site security

A later GAO investigation highlighted another vulnerability. In one special-access programme, a permanent government security representative was already located at the contractor's facility, yet classified document accountability still deteriorated.

According to GAO, the representative's workload and limited information-security experience meant that routine verification of document control was not sustained. The watchdog concluded that independent inspections by the Defence Investigative Service might have detected problems before accountability weakened. The broader lesson was that embedding security personnel inside a programme does not automatically substitute for periodic external review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Information Security: Need for DOD Inspections of Special Access Contracts &#124; U.S. GAO...</span></span></span>

### Concentrated authority creates governance pressure

Carve-outs intentionally combine operational knowledge and security administration inside the sponsoring programme office. That arrangement improves secrecy but also means the same organisation may oversee security compliance, approve access and manage programme execution simultaneously.

From a governance perspective, this concentration increases dependence on:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* accurate internal recordkeeping;
* disciplined document accountability;
* well-trained programme security personnel;
* periodic review by authorities outside the immediate programme whenever possible.

</div>

Government reviews consistently argue that these compensating controls are essential because the normal checks provided by standard industrial security oversight are reduced by design.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/ggd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ggd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) Contracts &#124; U.S. GAOFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

## Do carve-out contracts protect secrets or hide weaknesses?

The evidence suggests that carve-out contracts succeed at one very specific objective: preventing unnecessary disclosure that a contractor or facility participates in an exceptionally sensitive Special Access Program. For programmes where the existence of the contractual relationship itself would reveal important intelligence or military information, standard administrative visibility may indeed create unacceptable risk. Current regulations therefore continue to permit carve-outs only under tightly defined circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/32/117.6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute32 CFR § 117.6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Responsibilities. &#124; Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (e-CFR) &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

However, decades of government oversight show that the same features which strengthen secrecy can also weaken independent assurance if not carefully managed. Removing normal inspection channels increases reliance on the sponsoring programme's own security office, making documentation quality, inspection discipline and external validation proportionally more important. GAO reviews have consistently framed the issue not as whether carve-outs should exist, but whether exceptional secrecy is matched by equally robust and independent accountability mechanisms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/ggd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ggd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Further Improvements Needed in Department of Defense Oversight of Special Access (Carve-Out) Contracts &#124; U.S. GAOFebruary 18, 1983...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 1983</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
